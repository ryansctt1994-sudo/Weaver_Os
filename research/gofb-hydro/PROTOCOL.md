# GOFB-HYDRO-X001 — candidate protocol, not preregistered

Question: Do independently sampled natural waters differ in freezing behavior from synthetic major-ion-matched controls under standardized conditions?

Stage I:
1. Define independent sampling events and water sources; technical droplets do not count as independent source replicates.
2. Blind sample labels before freezing. Measure ions, pH, conductivity, dissolved gases, organic carbon, particle distribution and turbidity. Specify matching tolerances before examining outcomes.
3. Standardize vessels, droplet volume, contact surfaces, storage time and cooling ramp. Randomize positions and run order; calibrate thermal lag with reference samples.
4. Primary endpoint: freezing survival curve over temperature, including censored unfrozen droplets. Predefine aggregation by sampling event, analysis, exclusions and uncertainty.
5. Include DI, synthetic ion-match and synthetic ion-match plus specified particle controls. A residual is unexplained under measured controls, not proof of water memory.
6. Determine sample count by pilot-based power analysis before confirmatory collection. Freeze protocol, analysis code, tolerances and stopping rules before unblinding.
7. Filtration/heat/protease interventions require sham and reagent controls; treatment changes chemistry and particle distributions. A loss of activity alone does not uniquely identify a protein mechanism.

Stage II is conditional on Stage I characterization:
Test water state × acoustic geometry with calibrated field amplitude, thermal monitoring and sham treatment. Report interaction contrasts and distinguish streaming from radiation-force effects.
No physical or biological efficacy inference follows from simulator output.
